Water at the Bottom of Your Patio Door After Heavy Rain

Heavy rain and wind can expose blocked sill drainage, poor exterior grading, and worn seals near a patio door. This guide explains what to observe, what to avoid, and when a professional evaluation is appropriate.

Start with the symptoms

Inspect the bottom track for leaves and dirt after the rain stops. Look for drainage openings and check whether water sits inside the track or travels onto the interior floor.

What a repair professional will consider

A wet sill may stem from the door assembly, adjoining exterior flashing, or surrounding construction. The correct repair depends on where water enters.

Safety and common mistakes

Do not drill random holes into the threshold or fill existing drainage outlets with sealant. Doing so can worsen water intrusion.

Practical next step: Take photos during the next rain and note whether the leak occurs only with wind from one direction.

What this means for Michigan homeowners

Across Wayne, Oakland, and Macomb counties, seasonal moisture, freezing conditions, and everyday usage can affect door performance in different ways. Not every symptom is caused by winter weather, and the safest solution depends on the age, frame condition, and hardware installed. Keep notes about when the problem occurs and how it changes after simple cleaning. These details can make a service assessment more effective.

When to request service

If the sliding panel remains difficult to operate, its latch fails, the rail is visibly damaged, or a heavy glass panel appears misaligned, avoid forcing it. Request an assessment of the rollers, track, and related hardware.
